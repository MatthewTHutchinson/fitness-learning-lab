# Athletic skill and capacity

## Skill acquisition

- practice while attentive enough to detect and correct errors
- define success, not just attempts
- prefer a useful external effect cue when it improves the task
- use stable practice to establish a solution, then introduce variation to test
  retention and transfer
- separate technical failure from strength or mobility limitation

## Power and plyometrics

Power work is output practice. Use maximal intent, enough rest, and a contact or
set count that preserves the quality you are training. Landing and deceleration
are trainable skills, not merely the space between jumps.

Progress by increasing one demand: height/distance, speed, reactivity,
unilateral demand, direction, load, or complexity. Do not increase all at once.

## Mobility and length

Distinguish:

- available passive range
- active control in the range
- strength at long muscle lengths
- skill/confidence using the range in the target movement

Stretching, loaded strength through range, and skill practice answer different
problems. New range is more useful when followed by control and task exposure.

## Tendon and prehab work

Tendons respond to progressive mechanical loading, but symptoms and clinical
contexts vary. Build a continuum from tolerable strength work toward faster
energy-storage and sport demands. “Prehab” should name the capacity and its
progression, not become a permanent pile of light corrective drills.

Seek individualized clinical guidance for persistent/worsening pain, major
function loss, acute injury, neurological symptoms, or uncertainty about safe
loading.
