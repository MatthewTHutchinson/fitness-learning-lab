# Research summaries

These summaries are starting points, not substitutes for reading the papers.

## Resistance training prescription — ACSM position stand (2026)

- **Citation:** Currier et al., *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/), DOI:
  `10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897`.
- **Question:** Which resistance-training prescription variables affect
  strength, hypertrophy, power, balance, and function in healthy adults?
- **Evidence:** umbrella review supporting an updated position stand.
- **Practical implication:** consistent progressive resistance training matters
  more than chasing one universally optimal configuration; tailor load, volume,
  frequency, and exercise setup to the outcome and person.
- **Limitations:** broad recommendations aggregate populations and outcomes;
  they do not write an individual program.
- **Confidence:** high for general resistance-training principles; lower for a
  single “best” dose.

## Volume and frequency dose-response (2026)

- **Citation:** Pelland et al., *Sports Medicine*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/), DOI:
  `10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w`.
- **Question:** How do weekly set volume and frequency relate to strength and
  hypertrophy?
- **Practical implication:** volume and frequency should be treated as dose
  variables with diminishing returns, not binary rules. Frequency is also a way
  to distribute tolerable volume and practice.
- **Limitations:** meta-regression is observational across studies and cannot
  fully isolate every correlated program variable.
- **Confidence:** moderate.

## Proximity to failure (2024)

- **Citation:** Refalo et al., *Sports Medicine*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/), DOI:
  `10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2`.
- **Question:** How does estimated proximity to failure relate to strength and
  hypertrophy?
- **Practical implication:** effort is a dose variable, not a command to take
  every set to failure. Balance recruitment/stimulus against fatigue, technique,
  and the needs of subsequent work.
- **Limitations:** repetitions-in-reserve estimates and study protocols vary;
  exact thresholds are less certain than the broad relationship.
- **Confidence:** moderate.

## Resistance-training prescriptions network meta-analysis (2023)

- **Citation:** Currier et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/), DOI:
  `10.1136/bjsports-2023-106807`.
- **Question:** How do different load, set, and frequency combinations rank for
  strength and hypertrophy?
- **Practical implication:** many prescriptions work. Higher-load prescriptions
  tend to be more specific to maximal strength, while sufficient multi-set work
  supports hypertrophy.
- **Limitations:** network comparisons include indirect evidence and rankings
  can overstate practical separation between similar options.
- **Confidence:** moderate-high for broad patterns.

## Protein supplementation and resistance training (2018)

- **Citation:** Morton et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/), DOI:
  `10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608`.
- **Question:** Does protein supplementation add to resistance-training gains?
- **Practical implication:** adequate total protein supports adaptation, but
  benefit depends on baseline intake; powder is one delivery method.
- **Limitations:** supplementation trials vary in participants, training, total
  diet, and product.
- **Confidence:** high for the general role of adequate protein.

## Creatine position stand (2017)

- **Citation:** Kreider et al., *Journal of the International Society of Sports
  Nutrition*. [Full text](https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0173-z),
  DOI: `10.1186/s12970-017-0173-z`.
- **Practical implication:** creatine monohydrate is one of the better-supported
  performance supplements, especially alongside high-intensity training.
- **Limitations:** a position stand summarizes a large field; individual
  response and clinical context still matter.
- **Confidence:** high for healthy adults within studied use cases.

## Plyometric training in healthy adults (2019)

- **Citation:** Grgic et al., *Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in
  Sports*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31136014/), DOI:
  `10.1111/sms.13487`.
- **Practical implication:** plyometric training can improve jump, sprint, and
  strength-related outcomes. Dose and progression should preserve landing and
  output quality.
- **Limitations:** protocols and training status vary.
- **Confidence:** moderate-high for broad performance benefits.

## Resistance training and range of motion (2023)

- **Citation:** Alizadeh et al., *Sports Medicine*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36622555/), DOI:
  `10.1007/s40279-022-01804-x`.
- **Practical implication:** resistance training through suitable ranges can
  improve range of motion; mobility and strength programming need not be
  treated as opposites.
- **Limitations:** ROM measurement, exercise selection, and participants vary.
- **Confidence:** moderate.

## Tendon adaptation to mechanical loading (2015)

- **Citation:** Bohm et al., *Sports Medicine - Open*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27747846/), DOI:
  `10.1186/s40798-015-0009-9`.
- **Practical implication:** tendons adapt to chronic loading, and loading
  magnitude appears important. Progress tendon work as real training rather
  than endless unprogressed “prehab.”
- **Limitations:** healthy adults, mostly Achilles/patellar tendons; adaptation
  evidence is not a treatment prescription for an injured individual.
- **Confidence:** moderate.

## External attentional focus and motor learning (2021)

- **Citation:** Chua et al., *Psychological Bulletin*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34843301/), DOI:
  `10.1037/bul0000335`.
- **Practical implication:** cues about the intended movement effect often
  outperform body-part-focused cues for performance and learning. Test which
  cue produces the target behavior rather than repeating anatomy instructions.
- **Limitations:** tasks and populations vary; internal cues can still serve a
  diagnostic or teaching purpose.
- **Confidence:** moderate-high for the general attentional-focus effect.

## Concurrent aerobic and strength training (2012)

- **Citation:** Wilson et al., *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22002517/), DOI:
  `10.1519/JSC.0b013e31823a3e2d`.
- **Practical implication:** interference is affected by endurance mode,
  frequency, duration, and outcome. Manage dose and priority instead of
  assuming strength and conditioning cannot coexist.
- **Limitations:** older literature and heterogeneous protocols; newer reviews
  should be checked for a current narrow decision.
- **Confidence:** moderate for the broad tradeoff, lower for exact prescriptions.

## Velocity-based versus traditional loading (2022)

- **Citation:** Orange et al., *Journal of Sports Sciences*.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35380511/), DOI:
  `10.1080/02640414.2022.2059320`.
- **Practical implication:** velocity feedback can regulate training, but the
  available trials did not establish superior long-term outcomes over
  traditional loading. Use it when measurement changes a decision, not because
  it looks more scientific.
- **Limitations:** only four trials and 88 participants; estimates were
  imprecise and evidence quality low/very low.
- **Confidence:** moderate that superiority is unproven; low on fine details.
