# Programming framework catalogue

Frameworks are lenses. Use the least complicated one that improves a real
decision.

## Goal and adaptation frameworks

- **Needs analysis:** compare performance demands, current capacities, and
  constraints.
- **Goal hierarchy:** outcome → performance qualities → training adaptations →
  session actions.
- **Specificity:** prioritize adaptations and skills closest to the outcome.
- **Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio:** compare useful stimulus with local/systemic
  cost; context-dependent, not an intrinsic exercise score.
- **Fitness-fatigue / SRA:** training creates adaptations and fatigue on
  different time courses; use as a mental model, not a precise calculator.
- **Minimum effective dose:** start with the smallest dose that creates useful
  progress and adherence.

## Periodization and organization

- **Linear:** variables move broadly from general/high-volume toward
  specific/high-intensity.
- **Daily/weekly undulating:** rotate volume/intensity or emphasis more often.
- **Block / phase potentiation:** concentrate a limited set of qualities and
  sequence blocks so earlier work supports later work.
- **ATR:** accumulation, transmutation, realization; a block vocabulary used in
  sport preparation.
- **Conjugate/concurrent emphasis:** maintain several qualities while rotating
  the primary emphasis.
- **High/low:** consolidate high-intensity stress and protect low days.
- **Microdosing:** distribute small frequent exposures when skill, schedule, or
  fatigue makes long sessions undesirable.
- **Flexible nonlinear:** select among planned session types using readiness
  while preserving weekly priorities.

## Load and progression

- percentage-based loading
- RPE/RIR autoregulation
- APRE
- velocity-based training and velocity-loss stops
- double progression
- step, wave, and top-set/back-off loading
- load, volume, range, density, tempo, and complexity progressions

## Strength, hypertrophy, and power methods

- straight sets, supersets, circuits, clusters, rest-pause, drop sets
- eccentric emphasis, isometrics, accommodating resistance
- contrast/complex and French contrast training
- repetition-effort, maximal-strength, and dynamic-intent emphases
- full-range and long-muscle-length training

Methods are dose-delivery options. They are not adaptations by themselves.

## Skill and motor-learning frameworks

- blocked, serial, random, and interleaved practice
- whole-part-whole and regressions
- external/internal/holistic attentional focus
- constraints-led exploration
- deliberate practice, feedback bandwidth, retention and transfer tests

## Conditioning frameworks

- polarized, pyramidal, threshold-focused, and mixed intensity distributions
- Zone 2 / low-intensity volume
- tempo, threshold, HIIT, sprint interval training, and repeated-sprint work
- maximal aerobic speed/power prescriptions
- concurrent training and interference management

## Mobility and tendon frameworks

- static, dynamic, and PNF stretching
- loaded mobility and end-range strength
- CARs/control through range
- heavy slow resistance, eccentric and isometric loading
- energy-storage and plyometric progression
- symptom-monitoring and graded exposure

## Fatigue and competition frameworks

- planned deload, reactive deload, taper
- session RPE, monotony/strain, wellness tracking
- acute:chronic workload models (use cautiously; not injury prediction tools)
- maintain intensity while reducing volume before performance tests

## Selection rule

Before adopting a framework, answer:

1. What decision does it improve?
2. What extra data or complexity does it require?
3. What would I do differently because of it?
4. How will I know it helped?
