# Exercise selection without drowning in options

Exercises are tools. Choose one because it solves a programming problem better
than the alternatives available today.

## Selection scorecard

Score candidates qualitatively; do not pretend the numbers are precise.

| Criterion | Useful question |
|---|---|
| Specificity | Does the force, range, speed, coordination, or energy demand match the goal? |
| Trainability | Can I perform and load it consistently enough to learn from it? |
| Stimulus-to-fatigue | Does it create the desired work without consuming disproportionate recovery? |
| Tolerance | Can I repeat it with an acceptable symptom response? |
| Progression | Is there a clear next step for weeks, not just today? |
| Logistics | Does it fit my equipment, time, and gym flow? |
| Enjoyment/adherence | Will I actually practice it consistently? |

## Use a movement menu

For each recurring role, keep:

- one default exercise
- one low-readiness or low-equipment substitute
- one progression or higher-skill option

New saved movements enter the Programming Inbox. They earn a place in the menu
only after you can state their role, practice them, and identify what they would
replace. Adding without replacing is how programs become cluttered.

## Avoid false novelty

A variation is meaningfully different when it changes a decision-relevant
property: available range, stability demand, force direction, loadability,
velocity, coordination, tissue tolerance, or logistics. A new name or grip does
not automatically deserve a new program slot.

## Substitution rule

Match the role before matching the appearance. Replace a primary strength lift
with another loadable strength pattern; replace skill practice with an
appropriate regression; replace conditioning by matching the intended energy
and recovery demand.
