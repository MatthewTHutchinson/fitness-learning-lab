# Curriculum: what to learn, in order

The modules are ordered by dependency. Exercise variation comes after you can
name the adaptation and role; advanced periodization comes after you can run a
simple week and learn from it.

## Phase 1 — Build the decision language

1. **Adaptations and constraints**
   - strength, hypertrophy, power, speed, skill, mobility, tendon capacity,
     aerobic capacity, and fatigue resistance
   - performance goal versus training method
   - specificity, transfer, interference, recovery, and opportunity cost
2. **Movement roles**
   - primary outcome exercise
   - secondary builder
   - skill practice
   - accessory or tissue-capacity work
   - warm-up/preparation and conditioning
3. **Training dose**
   - load, reps, sets, frequency, rest, range of motion, tempo, proximity to
     failure, intent, contacts, duration, and density
   - which dose variables matter for which adaptation
4. **Exercise selection**
   - goal match, trainability, stimulus-to-fatigue ratio, pain/tolerance,
     equipment, skill cost, and progression path
   - selecting a small menu rather than collecting endless movements

## Phase 2 — Build sessions and weeks

5. **Session structure**
   - readiness check, preparation, skill/speed, power, strength, accessories,
     conditioning, and cooldown
   - ordering by quality and interference, not by a universal template
6. **Weekly structure**
   - exposure frequency, hard/easy distribution, spacing similar stressors,
     full-body versus splits, and concurrent training
7. **Progression**
   - double progression, load/repetition/volume progression, range-of-motion
     progression, density, complexity, and exercise progression
8. **Autoregulation**
   - RPE/RIR, rep quality, velocity loss, pain response, readiness, and
     minimum viable sessions

## Phase 3 — Learn the special cases

9. **Skill acquisition**
   - deliberate practice, feedback, external focus, blocked versus varied
     practice, retention, transfer, and fatigue management
10. **Power, speed, and plyometrics**
    - intent, force-velocity qualities, contacts, landing skill, rest, and
      keeping outputs high
11. **Mobility and length**
    - usable range, tolerance, passive range, active control, loaded length,
      and integrating new range into movement
12. **Tendon and joint capacity**
    - progressive loading, symptom response, heavy slow resistance,
      isometrics, energy-storage work, and return-to-performance progressions
13. **Conditioning**
    - aerobic base, thresholds, VO2-oriented intervals, sprint intervals,
      modality choice, and concurrent-training tradeoffs

## Phase 4 — Support and revise the system

14. **Recovery and fatigue**
    - sleep, energy availability, life stress, deloads, tapers, and distinguishing
      normal fatigue from a worsening problem
15. **Nutrition**
    - energy balance, protein, carbohydrate, fat, hydration, meal timing, and
      adjusting intake to training demands
16. **Supplements**
    - evidence, effect size, safety, third-party testing, opportunity cost, and
      when “not needed” is the correct answer
17. **Program review**
    - adherence, trend versus noise, leading and lagging indicators, what to
      keep, what to change, and changing one major variable at a time
18. **Research literacy**
    - population, comparison, outcome, duration, uncertainty, conflicts, and
      whether a statistically detectable effect is useful for your decision

## Independence milestones

- **Level 1:** explain why every exercise is in today’s session.
- **Level 2:** build a session from a program goal and a readiness constraint.
- **Level 3:** build and run a coherent week with explicit tradeoffs.
- **Level 4:** revise four to eight weeks of training from logs and outcomes.
- **Level 5:** read a review and decide whether it should change your practice.
